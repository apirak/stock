## Business Model
Designs and sells accelerated-computing platforms: data-center GPUs (Blackwell → Vera Rubin), Vera CPUs, networking (Spectrum-X, NVLink, BlueField), full rack-scale systems (NVL72), inference accelerators (Groq 3 LPX), plus the CUDA software ecosystem, AI enterprise software, and open models (Nemotron, Cosmos, GR00T). Fabless — manufacturing outsourced (TSMC, SK hynix memory). Sells to hyperscalers, cloud providers, sovereign AI programs, enterprises, and increasingly via financing partners funding AI-factory buildouts. (FACT)

## Revenue Drivers
1. **Data Center** — dominant: Q2 FY27 $89.0B of $96.2B total (~93%), +117% YoY (FACT)
2. Product cadence: Blackwell ramp → Vera Rubin in full production (Q2 FY27) (FACT)
3. Inference expansion (Groq 3 LPX full production, agentic AI) (FACT)
4. Sovereign AI (Japan national AI infra, Korea SK Telecom/NAVER partnerships) (FACT)
5. Edge/robotics/auto (DRIVE Hyperion, Jetson Thor) — small but optionality; Edge revenue $7.2B Q2 FY27, +27% YoY (FACT)
6. $500B+ third-party compute financing partnerships (Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, GS, KKR) mobilizing customer capex (FACT)

## Industry Structure
AI accelerators: NVDA holds dominant share of AI training/inference compute (ESTIMATE: ~80-90% of AI GPU market). Competitors: AMD (MI series), custom silicon (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Meta MTIA), Broadcom ASICs, Intel, and Chinese domestic (Huawei Ascend) where export rules block NVDA. Structural demand: AI infrastructure buildout — NVDA foresees $3–4T annual AI infra spend (company framing, OPINION/bull case). Customer concentration: a handful of hyperscalers + AI labs drive a large share of demand (FACT, general).

## Competitive Position
Full-stack platform: silicon + networking + systems + CUDA/software lock-in + annual cadence (Blackwell → Rubin → ...). MLPerf Training 6.0: Blackwell led every category (FACT). Moat reinforced by ecosystem switching costs and supply-chain scale (memory, CoWoS capacity). Threats: hyperscaler in-house ASICs for inference, price/perf competition, and China market loss due to export policy (Blackwell banned; Q3 FY27 guide assumes zero China DC compute revenue — FACT).

## Moat: **Strong**
Why: (1) CUDA/software ecosystem & developer lock-in (FACT, widely documented); (2) annual platform cadence competitors can't match at scale; (3) rack-scale systems + networking integration raises switching costs; (4) supply chain priority (HBM, advanced packaging). Caveat: moat is strongest in training; inference is more contestable (custom ASICs, Groq-style LPUs — which NVDA neutralized by licensing Groq for ~$20B, Dec 2025) (FACT).

## Major Risks
- **Business:** AI capex digestion/bubble — customers' AI ROI must materialize; circular financing risk in $500B partner-funded buildouts (OPINION)
- **Financial:** Margin compression (GM trough 71–72% guided; memory cost inflation); revenue concentration in few hyperscalers
- **Competitive:** Custom ASICs (TPU/Trainium), AMD, price/perf erosion in inference; Huawei in China
- **Regulatory:** US export controls (Blackwell banned to China; H20 licensing regime; 15% revenue-share precedent); potential cloud-access crackdown (CNBC 2026-08-19); antitrust scrutiny of Groq deal (Warren-Blumenthal Senate inquiry) (FACT)
- **Tech:** Architecture shifts (post-transformer paradigms), memory supply dependence (SK hynix/Samsung/Micron), TSMC concentration/Taiwan geopolitics
- **Management:** Key-man (Jensen Huang); Groq integration
- **Valuation:** Priced for continued hypergrowth; any growth disappointment compresses multiple sharply (see status file)

## Long-Term Investment Thesis (3–5 reasons)
1. AI compute demand is structural, multi-year; NVDA is the arms dealer to all sides, with $1T+ order visibility for Blackwell + Rubin (CEO, GTC Mar 2026) (FACT as stated by company)
2. Full-stack moat (silicon→networking→systems→CUDA) compounds; annual cadence keeps competitors perpetually behind (OPINION)
3. Inference/agentic AI is the next leg — Groq 3 LPX + Vera CPU extend the platform into latency-sensitive workloads (FACT products exist; impact ESTIMATE)
4. Financial machine: ~75% GM, ~66% op margin, $127B TTM FCF funding massive buybacks — per-share value compounds even if growth slows (FACT)
5. Sovereign AI + physical AI/robotics add demand layers beyond US hyperscalers (FACT deals announced; magnitude ESTIMATE)

## Thesis-Breaking Conditions (observable)
- Two consecutive quarters of sequential Data Center revenue **decline** without supply constraint excuse
- Hyperscaler/AI-lab capex guidance cuts (Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon, Oracle) or major order cancellations
- Gross margin sustained below ~65% (structural, not memory-cycle)
- CUDA/ecosystem erosion: material workload share moving to non-NVDA stacks (PyTorch-on-TPU parity, major lab defections)
- Export rules extended to block cloud access/all advanced SKUs globally for Chinese customers AND China demand not replaced
- Circular-financing unwind: AI-factory financing partners (Apollo/KKR structures) showing defaults or stranded capacity

## Relevant Valuation Methods
- **DCF (primary):** fits — massive, real, growing FCF; but terminal growth and AI-cycle duration dominate, so use scenario ranges not point values
- **Forward P/E / earnings power (secondary):** fits — profitable at scale; compare vs own history and mega-cap peers; apply conservative exit multiple on FY28–FY29 EPS
- **EV/FCF yield:** useful sanity check for "how much growth is priced in"
- NOT suitable: DDM (token dividend), P/B, asset-based. Sum-of-parts unnecessary (Data Center ~93% of revenue)
