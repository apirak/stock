## Business Model
Netflix operates a global subscription streaming service: a large owned-and-licensed library of TV series, films, documentaries, and mobile games delivered over the internet to ~190 countries. Customers pay a recurring monthly subscription (tiered by resolution/ads); since 2023 an ad-supported lower-priced tier adds advertising revenue. Revenue = memberships × price, plus a growing ads income stream. Revenue is ~99% recurring subscriptions. No inventory, negative working-capital cycle (~7 days cash conversion), content spend is the main "cost of revenue" (amortized). (FACT)

## Revenue Drivers
1. **Paid memberships** — net adds per quarter across ~190 countries (FACT)
2. **ARM (average revenue per membership)** — tier mix, price increases, ad-tier dilution (FACT, general)
3. **Advertising revenue** — ad-tier scale; key 2026 question is whether ads re-accelerate growth (ESTIMATE)
4. **Content slate cadence** — hits drive gross adds and churn reduction (FACT, general)
5. **Paid sharing monetization** — extra-member fees, largely harvested by 2025 (FACT, general)
6. **Content cost discipline** — amortization growth < revenue growth drives margin expansion (FACT trend: op margin 17.8% FY22 → 29.5% FY25)

## Industry Structure
Global streaming is a maturing oligopoly: Netflix, Disney+/Hulu, Max, Prime Video, YouTube, and regional players. Industry growth is shifting from subscriber acquisition to monetization (price/ads). U.S./Canada penetration is high — growth now from international markets, ad revenue, and pricing. Cyclicality is low (subscription revenue), but content tastes are hit-driven. Regulation: content quotas in some markets (EU 30% local-content rule), no existential regulatory overhang. Disruption risk: YouTube's free/paid hybrid and short-form attention competition (ESTIMATE — key structural question). (FACT/ESTIMATE mix)

## Competitive Position
- **Scale:** FY2025 revenue $45.18B — largest pure-play streamer by revenue and subscribers (FACT)
- **Content engine:** ~$16B+/year content amortization (implied, ESTIMATE from COGS) funds a global originals pipeline competitors struggle to match profitably
- **Distribution:** app pre-installs on virtually all connected devices; no middleman (FACT, general)
- **Data advantage:** 20+ years of viewing data guides content spend (OPINION)
- **Profitability gap:** Netflix is the only large streamer sustainably profitable at scale (FACT through FY2025)
- Threats: sports/live rights inflation, YouTube engagement, aggregation bundles (ESTIMATE)

## Moat: **Moderate-to-Strong**
Why: (1) Scale economics in content — per-viewer content cost falls with each added member; (2) global brand and habit ("default TV app"); (3) switching costs are low per se, but the watch-history/personalization layer raises them mildly; (4) profitability gap — rivals (Disney streaming, Max) still proving durable streaming profits while NFLX compounds FCF. Caveat: unlike NVDA-style lock-in, consumers hold multiple subscriptions and churn is one click; the moat is scale + habit + content ROI discipline, not structural captivity. (OPINION with FACTs cited)

## Major Risks
- **Business:** growth deceleration to ~11-12% with no visible next re-acceleration engine post-paid-sharing (FACT trend)
- **Financial:** FCF margin sensitive to content-cost discipline slipping (hit-driven overspending); FCF yield only ~2.7% at current price
- **Competitive:** YouTube's attention dominance; sports/live-rights inflation pulling dollars; bundling/aggregation weakening direct relationships
- **Regulatory:** local-content quotas (EU), modest; no existential overhang
- **Technology:** streaming is mature tech; risk is format shifts (short-form, interactive) rather than platform disruption
- **Management/governance:** reduced engagement-metric disclosure (2026) weakens outside visibility into content ROI — monitor carefully (FACT)
- **Valuation:** ~33x trailing diluted EPS, ~37x EV/FCF for a business guided to ~11-12% growth — multiple compression is the main downside path (FACT, computed)

## Long-Term Investment Thesis (3–5 reasons)
1. **Scale content economics:** largest subscriber base spreads the world's biggest content budget thinnest per viewer; flywheel of hits → adds → more budget (OPINION; scale FACT)
2. **Margin-expansion machine:** demonstrated 4 straight years of op-margin expansion (17.8%→29.5%, quarterly 33.4% in Q2'26) with minimal capex → structurally rising FCF (FACT)
3. **FCF fully returned:** ~$9.1B/yr buybacks shrink the share count — per-share value compounds even as growth normalizes (FACT)
4. **Ads + international runway:** ad tier and under-monetized markets (Asia, LATAM pricing) are real, if unproven, second-growth engines (FACT ad tier exists; magnitude ESTIMATE)
5. **Balance sheet:** net debt/EBITDA 0.18x, 17x interest coverage — no financial risk (FACT)

## Thesis-Breaking Conditions (observable)
- Revenue growth falls below ~8% for two consecutive quarters WITHOUT ad revenue inflecting
- Operating margin stalls or declines for 2+ quarters due to content-cost re-acceleration or sports-rights splurge
- FCF declines for two consecutive years while buybacks continue at full pace (quality-of-return deterioration)
- Subscriber churn spike in UCAN/EMEA from price increases or a credible bundled competitor taking share
- Further disclosure reductions (e.g., dropping membership reporting) combined with guidance misses — governance red flag
- Debt rising materially above 1.5x EBITDA to fund content

## Relevant Valuation Methods
- **DCF (primary):** fits — real, growing FCF (~$9.5B) with visible margin runway; use scenario ranges because terminal growth and ad-tier outcome dominate
