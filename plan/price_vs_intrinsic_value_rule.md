# Price vs Intrinsic Value Rule

A falling stock price does NOT automatically mean the stock is more attractive.

Always compare:

- Change in market price
- Change in estimated intrinsic value

Example:

Price: -15%
Intrinsic Value: -2%
→ Margin of Safety improved materially
→ Possible Temporary Mispricing Candidate

Price: -30%
Intrinsic Value: -40%
→ The stock may actually be LESS attractive
→ Possible Value Trap Risk

Never classify a stock as attractive based only on price decline.

---

# Revaluation Discipline

Do NOT rebuild DCF or intrinsic value estimates every day just because the stock price changed.

Revalue only when material assumptions change, such as:

- Revenue growth outlook
- Margin outlook
- Free cash flow
- Capital expenditure
- Competitive position
- Moat
- Long-term demand
- Cost of capital
- Share count
- Debt
- Management guidance
- Structural industry changes

Daily price movement should update Margin of Safety, NOT automatically change intrinsic value.

If assumptions have not materially changed:

Keep the previous intrinsic value range
Update only:
- Current price
- Margin of Safety
- Opportunity Status
- Research Priority if necessary
