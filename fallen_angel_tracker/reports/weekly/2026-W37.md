## Section 3 — Buffett-style Mini-Lesson

### 📚 Chipotle's E. Coli Outbreak (2015)

When Chipotle's E. coli scandal hit in 2015, the stock roughly halved and headlines declared fast-casual dead. But look at what actually broke: nothing structural. Units stayed profitable, debt was zero, and the reason customers chose Chipotle — fast, fresh, customizable food — was untouched by a supply-chain contamination at one ingredient. Comparable sales recovered within a year and the stock went on to triple. The Buffett filter is simple: a crisis that damages the CUSTOMER'S REASON FOR CHOOSING you is structural; a crisis that merely damages their CONFIDENCE for a season is transitory. The investor's job in the panic is not optimism — it is verification. Walk the stores, read the unit economics, check the balance sheet. If the organs are healthy, the wound is a discount. Actionable takeaway: when a fallen angel scares the market, ask one question — did the crisis remove the reason customers choose this business, or only their courage to admit it in public?
